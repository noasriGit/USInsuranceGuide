  Overview: |
    D.C. homeowners insurance, also searched as Washington DC home insurance, is a package policy for people who own a house, rowhouse, or condo unit. [DISB](https://disb.dc.gov/page/insurance-tips-homeowners-and-renters) publishes consumer tips for homeowners and renters.

    Compare [Maryland homeowners insurance](/states/maryland/homeowners-insurance/) and [Virginia homeowners insurance](/states/virginia/homeowners-insurance/) if you own across the line. Condo unit owners should start with [condo insurance](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/). Tenants should use [D.C. renters insurance](/states/washington-dc/renters-insurance/).
  Coverage Options to Consider: |
    Typical HO-3 policies include dwelling, other structures, personal property, loss of use, personal liability, and medical payments.

    **Replacement cost versus actual cash value.** Older D.C. rowhouses and renovated interiors can cost more to rebuild than a tax assessment suggests. Set the dwelling limit from rebuild cost.

    **Condo versus house.** An HO-6 sits beside the association master policy. It is not an HO-3 with a different label. See [condo insurance](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/).

    **Usually excluded:** Flood, earthquake, sewer backup unless endorsed, and gradual maintenance damage. Flood requires a separate policy. See [home insurance versus flood insurance](/blog/home-insurance-vs-flood-insurance/).

    **Water and sewer backup.** Basement and below-grade units near the Potomac or Anacostia, and older drain lines, make this endorsement worth a specific conversation.

    **Landlord versus homeowner.** If you rent the property to others, you generally need [landlord insurance](/states/washington-dc/landlord-insurance/), not a standard owner-occupied HO-3.
  State Requirements & Regulations: |
    The District does not mandate homeowners insurance by statute. Lenders may require coverage for mortgaged properties and may force-place a policy if yours lapses.

    DISB publishes consumer guides, handles complaints, and recommends verifying that insurers are licensed in D.C. before purchasing.

    Properties near the Potomac, Anacostia, and other low-lying areas may face flood exposure that a homeowners policy does not cover. Check a flood map even for urban lots.
  Factors That May Affect Your Premium: |
    Location, dwelling age and construction, coverage limits, deductibles, claims history, protective devices, and credit-based insurance scores may affect premium.

    We do not publish invented D.C. average homeowners premiums or name a cheapest carrier. Compare licensed insurers on the same dwelling limit, deductible, and wind or water endorsements. Bundling auto and home, or raising a deductible, may change cost. Discuss options with a licensed agent.
faq:
  - question: "Is homeowners insurance required in Washington, D.C.?"
    answer: "Not by District law, but mortgage lenders typically require it. Without a mortgage, coverage is optional but may protect your home and liability exposure."
  - question: "Does D.C. homeowners insurance cover floods?"
    answer: "Standard policies typically exclude flood damage. Separate flood insurance through the NFIP or a private insurer may be needed."
  - question: "What does a standard D.C. homeowners policy cover?"
    answer: "Most HO-3 policies cover the dwelling and other structures against open perils, personal property against named perils, liability, and loss of use, subject to limits and exclusions."
  - question: "Should a D.C. condo owner buy homeowners insurance?"
    answer: "Condo owners typically need an HO-6 unit-owner policy plus the association master policy, not a standard HO-3. Lenders and associations often require the HO-6 even though District law does not."
