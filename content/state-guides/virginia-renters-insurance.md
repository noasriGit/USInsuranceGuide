  Coverage Options to Consider: |
    **Personal property.** Furniture, electronics, and clothing against covered perils such as fire and theft. Replacement cost versus actual cash value changes claim value.

    **Personal liability.** Injury in your rental or damage you cause to someone else's property. Lease-required limits are contract terms, not a Virginia insurance statute.

    **Loss of use.** Temporary living expenses after a covered loss that makes the unit uninhabitable.

    **Water versus flood.** Sudden interior water may be covered. Flood is typically excluded. Separate contents flood coverage may matter near streams, tidal creeks, or low-lying Northern Virginia sites. See [home insurance versus flood insurance](/blog/home-insurance-vs-flood-insurance/).

    **Roommates and students.** Unrelated roommates are not automatically covered. Students should confirm whether a parent's policy lists the Virginia address.

    **Pets.** Animal liability depends on the policy. Some leases and carriers treat dog liability separately.
  State Requirements & Regulations: |
    Virginia does not require renters insurance by state law. Many landlords include it as a lease requirement. Details are on [Virginia renters insurance requirements](/states/virginia/renters-insurance/requirements/).

    Arlington County and the City of Fairfax do not create a different renters statute. They add housing type and commute context on top of the same statewide rule.

    The Bureau of Insurance regulates insurers and can help with licensing questions and complaints.

  - question: "What does Virginia renters insurance cover?"
    answer: "A standard HO-4 policy typically covers personal property, personal liability, medical payments to others, and loss of use, not the building structure."
  - question: "Does renters insurance cover flood damage in Virginia?"
    answer: "Standard renters policies typically exclude flood. Separate flood insurance for contents may be available in participating communities."
  - question: "Do roommates need separate renters policies?"
    answer: "Generally yes. Each person needs their own policy or must be named on the same policy to be covered."
