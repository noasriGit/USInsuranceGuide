  Overview: |
    Renters insurance Maryland searches usually want two answers: is it required, and what does it cover. The short version is that Maryland law does not mandate an HO-4 policy, and a standard policy may cover personal property, liability, and additional living expenses after a covered loss.

    The full statute-versus-lease explanation is on [is renters insurance required in Maryland](/states/maryland/renters-insurance/requirements/). Local rental context is on [Silver Spring renters insurance](/states/maryland/silver-spring/). Compare [Virginia renters insurance](/states/virginia/renters-insurance/) and [D.C. renters insurance](/states/washington-dc/renters-insurance/) if you are moving across the DMV.

    The [Maryland Insurance Administration](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/pages/homeownersandrentersinsurance.aspx) publishes consumer guides on renters coverage.
  Coverage Options to Consider: |
    **Personal property.** Furniture, electronics, clothing, and other belongings against named covered perils such as fire and theft, subject to limits, sublimits, and the deductible. Replacement cost versus actual cash value changes how a claim is valued.

    **Personal liability.** If someone is injured in your rental or you damage someone else's property. Landlords often set a minimum liability limit in the lease. That is a contract rule, not a Maryland statute.

    **Loss of use.** Hotel and related costs if a covered loss makes the unit uninhabitable.

    **Water versus flood.** Sudden water from a burst pipe or appliance may be covered. Flood, meaning rising water or surface water, is typically excluded. Separate contents flood coverage may be available. See [home insurance versus flood insurance](/blog/home-insurance-vs-flood-insurance/).

    **Pets.** Dog or other animal liability is policy-specific. Some carriers exclude certain breeds or require an endorsement. Ask before you assume a bite or injury claim is covered.

    **Condo rentals.** If you rent a condo, you still generally need an HO-4. The unit owner's HO-6 and the association master policy are not a substitute for a tenant's belongings. See [condo insurance](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/).
  State Requirements & Regulations: |
    Maryland does not require renters insurance by state law. Many landlords include it as a lease requirement with minimum liability limits and proof at move-in. Details and roommate rules are on [Maryland renters insurance requirements](/states/maryland/renters-insurance/requirements/).

    Each roommate generally needs their own policy unless all are named on one policy. A parent's policy may or may not extend to a Maryland college address. Confirm the listed location and named insureds.

    The MIA regulates insurers and handles consumer complaints. Verify that a company is licensed in Maryland before you buy.
  Factors That May Affect Your Premium: |
    Personal property limits, deductible, location, claims history, and optional endorsements such as scheduled items or sewer backup may affect cost. Downtown Silver Spring apartments can rate differently from Eastern Shore rentals. That is a rating difference, not a different legal requirement.

    We do not publish invented average Maryland renters premiums. Compare licensed insurers on the same limits and deductible. Do not drop liability below a lease minimum just to save money if the lease requires a higher figure.

  - question: "Does renters insurance cover flood damage in Maryland?"
    answer: "Standard renters policies typically exclude flood. Separate flood insurance for contents may be available in participating communities, which matters for low-lying and coastal areas."

    answer: "Generally yes. Each person needs their own policy or must be named on the same policy to be covered."
