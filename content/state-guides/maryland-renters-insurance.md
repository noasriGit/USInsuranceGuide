  Overview: |
    Renters insurance Maryland searches usually want two answers: is it required, and what does it cover. The short version is that Maryland law does not mandate an HO-4 policy, and a standard policy may cover personal property, liability, and additional living expenses after a covered loss.

    The full statute-versus-lease explanation is on [is renters insurance required in Maryland](/states/maryland/renters-insurance/requirements/). Local rental context is on [Silver Spring renters insurance](/states/maryland/silver-spring/). Compare [Virginia renters insurance](/states/virginia/renters-insurance/) and [D.C. renters insurance](/states/washington-dc/renters-insurance/) if you are moving across the DMV.

    The [Maryland Insurance Administration](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/pages/homeownersandrentersinsurance.aspx) publishes consumer guides on renters coverage. Maryland does not assign one official renters premium. Cost factors and how to compare equivalent quotes are in the section below.
  Coverage Options to Consider: |
    **Personal property.** Furniture, electronics, clothing, and other belongings against named covered perils such as fire and theft, subject to limits, sublimits, and the deductible. Replacement cost versus actual cash value changes how a claim is valued.

    **Personal liability.** If someone is injured in your rental or you damage someone else's property. Landlords often set a minimum liability limit in the lease. That is a contract rule, not a Maryland statute.

    **Loss of use.** Hotel and related costs if a covered loss makes the unit uninhabitable.

    **Water versus flood.** Sudden water from a burst pipe or appliance may be covered. Flood, meaning rising water or surface water, is typically excluded. Separate contents flood coverage may be available. See [home insurance versus flood insurance](/blog/home-insurance-vs-flood-insurance/).

    **Pets.** Dog or other animal liability is policy-specific. Some carriers exclude certain breeds or require an endorsement. Ask before you assume a bite or injury claim is covered.

    **Condo rentals.** If you rent a condo, you still generally need an HO-4. The unit owner's HO-6 and the association master policy are not a substitute for a tenant's belongings. See [condo insurance](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/).
  State Requirements & Regulations: |
    Maryland does not require renters insurance by state law. Many landlords include it as a lease requirement with minimum liability limits and proof at move-in. Details and roommate rules are on [Maryland renters insurance requirements](/states/maryland/renters-insurance/requirements/).

    Each roommate generally needs their own policy unless all are named on one policy. A parent's policy may or may not extend to a Maryland college address. Confirm the listed location and named insureds.

    The MIA regulates insurers and handles consumer complaints. Verify that a company is licensed in Maryland before you buy.
  Factors That May Affect Your Premium: |
    ### How much does renters insurance cost in Maryland?

    Maryland does not publish one official renters insurance price that applies to every tenant. What you pay depends on the coverage you buy, where you live, the building, your claims history, and optional endorsements. A lease-required liability limit can also change the quote. That lease figure is a contract floor, not a Maryland statute.

    The [Maryland Insurance Administration](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/pages/homeownersandrentersinsurance.aspx) publishes a [Renters Insurance: A Comparison Guide to Rates](https://insurance.maryland.gov/consumer/documents/publicnew/renters-insurance-rate-guide.pdf) (as of August 2026). That guide lists **sample annual premiums by ZIP code and insurer** for stated scenario assumptions: frame construction, replacement-cost contents, and a $500 deductible. ZIP codes are for sampling only. Sample contents and liability limits also differ by locality in the guide. Those figures are comparison scenarios. They are not your quote, not a statewide average, and not a cheapest-insurer ranking.

    **Rating factors that commonly move a Maryland renters quote:**

    | Factor | Why it can change premium |
    |---|---|
    | Personal property limit | A higher contents limit generally costs more because the insurer is promising a larger payout after a covered loss. |
    | Liability limit | A lease may require a minimum. Raising liability above that floor usually costs more. |
    | Deductible | A higher deductible usually lowers premium and raises what you pay after a property claim. |
    | Replacement cost vs actual cash value | Replacement cost generally pays to replace items with similar new items. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation and often quotes lower. |
    | ZIP code and building type | Downtown Silver Spring apartments can rate differently from Eastern Shore rentals. Construction, fire protection, and theft exposure can differ. |
    | Claims history | Prior losses can affect price and eligibility. |
    | Endorsements | Scheduled jewelry, sewer backup, or similar add-ons add premium because they add coverage. |

    Compare licensed insurers on the same property limit, liability limit, deductible, and valuation (replacement cost or actual cash value). Do not drop liability below a lease minimum just to save money if the lease requires a higher figure. For the legal question of whether coverage is required, use [Maryland renters insurance requirements](/states/maryland/renters-insurance/requirements/).

  - question: "How much is renters insurance in Maryland?"
    answer: "There is no single official Maryland renters premium. Price depends on personal property limits, liability limits, deductibles, replacement cost versus actual cash value, ZIP code, building type, claims history, and endorsements.   - question: "Is renters insurance required in Maryland?"
    answer: "Not by state law, but your lease may require it. Even when optional, it may protect your belongings and liability. Your landlord's policy does not cover your possessions."
  - question: "Can a Maryland landlord require renters insurance?"
    answer: "Yes. A lease is a private contract. Many Maryland landlords require a renters policy, a minimum liability limit, and proof of coverage. That is a contract rule, not a statewide Insurance Administration mandate. Details are on the Maryland renters insurance requirements page."
  - question: "What affects the cost of renters insurance in Maryland?"
    answer: "Insurers commonly consider the contents limit, liability limit, deductible, replacement cost versus actual cash value, ZIP code, building type, claims history, and optional endorsements. Two tenants in the same county can receive different quotes for the same coverage."
  - question: "Does renters insurance cover roommates?"
    answer: "Generally no, not automatically. Each person needs their own policy or must be named on the same policy to be covered."
  - question: "Does renters insurance cover flood damage?"
    answer: "Standard renters policies typically exclude flood. Separate flood insurance for contents may be available in participating communities, which matters for low-lying and coastal areas."
  - question: "What does Maryland renters insurance cover?"
    answer: "A standard HO-4 policy typically covers personal property, personal liability, medical payments to others, and loss of use, not the building structure."
