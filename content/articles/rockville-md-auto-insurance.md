If you register a vehicle in Rockville, Maryland, you must meet Maryland auto insurance rules: 30/60/15 liability, matching uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage, and a personal injury protection offer of at least $2,500 that you may waive in writing. Rockville is the Montgomery County seat. City and county websites do not replace those statewide rules.

I-270 and Metro Red Line commuting shape how people use a car here. They do not create Rockville-only limits, and this page does not invent Rockville premiums. For the legal floor, use the [Maryland auto insurance overview](/states/maryland/auto-insurance/) and the [Maryland auto insurance requirements guide](/states/maryland/auto-insurance/requirements/). Cost context, without treating any figure as a Rockville average, lives on the [Maryland auto insurance cost page](/states/maryland/auto-insurance/cost/).

## County seat, I-270, and a mixed parking pattern

Rockville combines a downtown and Town Center core with neighborhoods of single-family homes and apartment communities. Many trips follow I-270 toward the Beltway or use the Red Line at Rockville, Twinbrook, or nearby Shady Grove. Some households garage a car in a driveway. Others park in apartment garages or surface lots and use Metro for the work trip.

That mix is the local difference. It is not a separate insurance code. The [City of Rockville website](https://www.rockvillemd.gov/) and [Montgomery County website](https://www.montgomerycountymd.gov/) explain local services. The [Maryland Insurance Administration](https://insurance.maryland.gov/) and [Maryland MVA insurance requirements](https://mva.maryland.gov/title-registration/auto-insurance-requirements) explain what a registered vehicle must carry.

Compared with [Bethesda auto insurance](/states/maryland/bethesda/), Rockville sits farther up the corridor. Bethesda is closer to the District, with denser mixed-use blocks and more high-rise or condo-adjacent parking. Same Maryland statute. Different garaging and commute stories on the application.

If you also rent, Maryland renters rules are separate. See [Maryland renters insurance](/states/maryland/renters-insurance/) and, for a more renter-heavy nearby market, [Silver Spring renters insurance](/states/maryland/silver-spring/).

## Maryland rules that actually apply

Maryland requires every registered vehicle to carry a policy from a company licensed in the state.

| Coverage | Minimum limit |
|---|---|
| Bodily injury, one person | $30,000 |
| Bodily injury, per accident | $60,000 |
| Property damage, per accident | $15,000 |

Those limits are written 30/60/15. UM/UIM bodily injury and uninsured motorist property damage minimums match them. You may buy higher UM/UIM limits. You should not assume the legal floor will cover a serious injury claim.

Insurers must offer at least $2,500 in PIP. PIP can pay certain medical expenses and lost wages regardless of fault, up to the limit you buy. You may:

- Keep full PIP
- Choose limited PIP, which can exclude benefits for you and household members age 16 and over
- Sign an affirmative written waiver on the state-required form

A waiver is a real decision. Health insurance does not always fill the same gaps, and PIP does not wait for a fault finding. Read the form. Ask what it changes before you sign it.

Maryland is generally an at-fault state for bodily injury liability claims. PIP sits beside liability. It does not replace it. Virginia, by contrast, uses a 50/100/25 liability floor for current policies and does not require a PIP offer. If you work in Virginia, read [Virginia auto insurance requirements](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/requirements/) and [Alexandria auto insurance](/states/virginia/alexandria/) as a contrast. A Rockville-registered car still has to satisfy Maryland.

## Electronic verification and lapse fees

MVA verifies insurance electronically. When you register or renew, you certify that coverage will stay in force. If the record goes dark, you can face:

- $150 for the first 30 days without verified insurance
- $7 for each additional day
- Registration suspension
- An FR-19 certificate from your insurer showing continuous coverage

Insurers must provide FR-19 forms to policyholders without a fee. Carry proof of insurance when you drive. If you change carriers, ask both companies how the electronic file is updated so a switch does not look like a gap.

This page does not invent extra Rockville penalties. The fees above are statewide Maryland rules.

## What I-270 and Red Line commuting change

They change the facts you put on an application, not the statute:

- Annual mileage may be higher if most workdays are I-270 corridor drives
- Mileage may be lower if the car sits near a Red Line station most weekdays
- Driveway garaging, apartment-garage garaging, and street or lot parking are different answers
- Collision and comprehensive become a practical question if the car is financed, new, or parked in a shared facility
- Rental reimbursement may matter if you need the vehicle for a corridor commute while it is repaired

State law does not require collision or comprehensive. A lender often does. Do not copy a Bethesda parking story onto a Rockville driveway, or the reverse.

Flood is not solved by auto comprehensive. If the question is rising water at a house or apartment, use [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/). Comprehensive may respond to certain weather damage to the vehicle. It is not a homeowners or renters flood policy.

## Coverage combinations that matter in Rockville

Discuss these combinations with a licensed Maryland producer. They are not Rockville premiums:

- Liability and UM/UIM at or above 30/60/15, based on assets and how you drive I-270 or local roads
- Full PIP, limited PIP, or a written waiver
- Collision and comprehensive matched to driveway versus apartment parking
- Deductibles you can pay after a lot or garage incident
- Accurate garaging address inside Rockville, not a work address in another jurisdiction
- Coordinating auto with a renters or homeowners policy if you also live in an apartment or single-family home
