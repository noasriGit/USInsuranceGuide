Virginia does not set one official homeowners insurance price. Premiums vary by location, construction, replacement cost, deductibles, claims history, and other rating factors. **State law does not require homeowners insurance.** A mortgage lender typically does, and that contract rule is separate from the insurance code.

| Topic | What it means for cost |
|---|---|
| State-law requirement | Virginia does not mandate a homeowners policy by statute. |
| Lender or association rule | A mortgage or condo association can still require coverage. |
| Main price drivers | Location, wind exposure, rebuild cost, deductibles, claims, and credit-based scores where permitted. |
| Replacement-cost guidance | The Bureau of Insurance recommends dwelling limits of at least 80% of replacement cost. |
| Flood | Usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. |

For coverages and regional risk notes, see [Virginia homeowners insurance](/states/virginia/homeowners-insurance/). Maryland uses the same state-law versus lender split. See [Maryland homeowners insurance](/states/maryland/homeowners-insurance/) if you are comparing a move across the line.

## Virginia State Law Versus Lender Requirements

**Virginia statute** does not require a homeowner to buy homeowners insurance. Owning a home free and clear does not trigger a state-issued homeowners mandate.

**Mortgage lenders** typically require a policy for the life of the loan. The lender is usually listed as mortgagee. Dwelling coverage must meet the lender's minimum, which may reference the loan balance or replacement cost. If the policy lapses, the lender may force-place insurance. Force-placed coverage is often more expensive and more limited than a policy you shop yourself.

**Condominium associations** may require an HO-6 unit owners policy even when a master policy covers the building shell. That is an association or bylaw requirement. See [condo insurance](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/) for the unit-owner versus homeowners form difference.

The [Virginia Bureau of Insurance](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/consumers/insurance/) publishes consumer guidance and can help with complaints. It does not assign you a statewide premium.

## What Affects Virginia Homeowners Insurance Rates

Insurers price the chance they will pay for dwelling repair, contents, additional living expenses, or liability. Company rating plans differ. Common factors include:

| Factor | How it can move premium |
|---|---|
| Location | Fire protection, crime, building codes, and nearby claims experience differ across Northern Virginia, Tidewater, and inland counties. |
| Coastal or wind exposure | Chesapeake, Atlantic, and tidal properties may see higher wind pricing or percentage wind deductibles. |
| Construction and age | Older roofs, wiring, or non-standard construction can change rebuild cost and underwriting. |
| Replacement cost | A higher dwelling limit generally costs more because the insurer is promising a larger rebuild payment. |
| Deductibles | A higher deductible usually lowers premium and raises your share after a claim. |
| Claims history | Prior losses on the property or the policyholder can affect price and eligibility. |
| Credit-based insurance scores | Where permitted, these scores may be one rating variable. |
| Protective devices | Alarms, sprinklers, and smoke detection may qualify for company-specific discounts. |

Northern Virginia homes in [Arlington](/states/virginia/arlington/) and [Fairfax](/states/virginia/fairfax/) often have higher rebuild costs than the purchase-price conversation implies. Coastal and river properties can add wind and flood questions that a mountain or Piedmont home does not share. Those are rating and underwriting facts, not published average premiums.

## Replacement Cost Versus Actual Cash Value in Virginia

Valuation is one of the largest cost-and-gap choices on a Virginia homeowners quote.

| Valuation method | What the insurer generally pays |
|---|---|
| Replacement cost | Repair or replace with similar materials at current prices, without subtracting depreciation (subject to limits and deductibles). |
| Actual cash value (ACV) | Replacement cost minus depreciation. |

Replacement cost coverage usually costs more than ACV. ACV can look cheaper when you buy the policy and then leave a shortfall when you rebuild.

The Virginia Bureau of Insurance recommends dwelling limits of at least **80% of replacement cost** to reduce the chance of a coinsurance problem at claim time. That is consumer guidance about limit adequacy. It is not a statutory premium, and it is not a reason to invent a statewide average rate.

Set the dwelling limit from a **rebuild-cost estimate**, not from the tax assessment, the remaining mortgage, or last year's purchase price. Construction costs in Northern Virginia can move faster than a loan balance.

Contents can also be replacement cost or ACV. Two quotes are not equivalent if one depreciates your furniture and the other does not.

## Coastal Wind, Northern Virginia Rebuild Cost, and Deductibles

Virginia is not one rating territory.

**Tidewater and coastal properties** may carry wind or hurricane deductibles written as a **percentage of dwelling coverage** rather than a flat dollar amount. Ask whether that deductible applies to all wind or only to named storms, and calculate the dollar amount before you treat the quote as inexpensive.

**Northern Virginia** pricing often tracks rebuild labor, materials, and higher dwelling limits. An Arlington townhouse and a Fairfax single-family home can insure very differently even when the owners have similar credit and claims history.

**Inland and mountain regions** still face winter storms, ice, and plumbing freeze claims. Those show up in claims history and in whether you add water-backup endorsements.

A deductible you cannot pay is not a savings. It is a claim-time bill.

## Flood Exclusion and Separate Flood Insurance

A standard homeowners policy typically **excludes flood**. Flood generally means water that rises from the ground or overflows from a body of water, including storm surge and surface water. Hampton Roads, tidal rivers, and many inland low-lying areas share that gap.

Flood insurance is a separate product, often through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood insurer. [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) explains building versus contents coverage and typical waiting periods. For the policy contrast, see [home insurance versus flood insurance](/blog/home-insurance-vs-flood-insurance/) and the [flood insurance hub](/flood-insurance/).

A federally related mortgage on a property in a mapped high-risk flood zone can require flood insurance even though Virginia does not require a homeowners policy by statute. Do not drop flood coverage from escrow just to make a homeowners quote look lower.

## Condo Insurance Versus Homeowners Insurance in Virginia

An HO-3 style homeowners policy prices the dwelling. An HO-6 condo policy prices interior improvements, personal property, and liability. The association master policy usually covers shared building elements.

That split changes any cost comparison:

- Do not line up a condo quote against a single-family dwelling quote
- Ask for the master-policy deductible and any loss-assessment exposure
- Confirm association-required liability or loss-assessment amounts

Use the [condo insurance guide](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/) when you own a unit. Use this page when you need dwelling coverage on a house or townhome.

## Ways to Reduce a Virginia Homeowners Premium Without Creating Gaps

You can change inputs the insurer actually rates. You should not thin out coverages you still need.

Discuss, without invented savings percentages:

- **A deductible you can fund** after a fire or windstorm
- **Protective devices** the company credits
- **Multi-policy pricing** if you also place auto coverage with the same insurer
- **An updated rebuild-cost worksheet** so the dwelling limit matches the house
- **Roof, plumbing, or electrical updates** the underwriter recognizes

Avoid "savings" that create a gap:

- Cutting the dwelling limit below a realistic rebuild number
- Switching to ACV without understanding depreciation
- Ignoring a percentage wind deductible
- Assuming flood or sewer backup is included
- Allowing a lapse that leads to force-placed insurance

The Bureau of Insurance consumer pages are for shopping questions and complaints, not for unofficial carrier rankings. This site does not call any company cheapest or best.

## How to Compare Virginia Homeowners Quotes

1. Start with a current **rebuild-cost estimate**.
2. Align dwelling, other structures, contents, loss of use, and liability.
3. Align **all-peril and wind deductibles**, including any percentage wind deductible converted to dollars.
4. List the same **endorsements** on every quote if you need them.
5. Confirm each company is **licensed in Virginia**.
6. Read flood, earth-movement, and maintenance-related water exclusions.

If one quote is much lower, find the missing limit or the ACV clause before you bind. For help lining up equivalent specifications, [get insurance help](/get-insurance-help/).

## Questions to Ask a Licensed Professional About Virginia Homeowners Cost

- Is my dwelling limit at least 80% of current replacement cost, and is that enough for this house?
- What is my wind or hurricane deductible in dollars?
- Am I comparing replacement cost to actual cash value without realizing it?
- What does my lender or association require that Virginia law does not require?
- Do I need separate flood insurance for this address?
- If I own a condo, should I be shopping an HO-6 instead of a homeowners form?

A licensed Virginia agent or broker can price the same rebuild-cost number across companies and separate a real discount from a thinner policy.
