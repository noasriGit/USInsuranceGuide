If you rent in Arlington County, Virginia, renters insurance is not required by state statute, but many high-rise and garden-apartment leases still require it. Your policy is a Virginia HO-4 contract. D.C. insurance rules do not apply to an Arlington unit, even if you work in the District or ride Metro across the river every day.

Arlington is a dense inner-core county, not a neighborhood of Washington. The [Virginia Bureau of Insurance](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/consumers/insurance/) regulates the policy. [Arlington County's website](https://www.arlingtonva.us/) can help with local services, but it does not write insurance law. This page explains what is locally different about renting here, then points you to the statewide guides and official sources.

For the broader Virginia rules, start with the [Virginia renters insurance overview](/states/virginia/renters-insurance/) and the [Virginia renters insurance requirements guide](/states/virginia/renters-insurance/requirements/). If you also keep a car in the county, pair this page with [Virginia auto insurance](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/).

## Arlington is a Virginia county, not a D.C. rental market

A large share of Arlington households rent. The housing stock is stacked and transit-oriented: high-rises along the Orange, Silver, and Blue Metro corridors, plus garden apartments and smaller walk-ups in other neighborhoods.

If you previously rented in the District, do not assume your old DISB-oriented advice still applies. Compare this page with [Washington, D.C. renters insurance](/states/washington-dc/renters-insurance/). The building may sit a few Metro stops from downtown Washington. The insurance regulator does not.

People who later move to [Fairfax renters insurance](/states/virginia/fairfax/) or [Silver Spring renters insurance](/states/maryland/silver-spring/) will meet a different housing pattern and, in Maryland, a different regulator.

## What a standard renters policy is for in Arlington

A landlord's policy covers the building. It does not replace your furniture, electronics, clothing, or bicycles, and it does not automatically defend you if a guest is injured in your unit or water from your bathtub damages a neighbor below you.

A typical HO-4 renters policy may include:

- Personal property coverage for belongings against named perils such as fire, theft, and certain sudden water damage from plumbing inside the unit
- Loss of use, which may help pay temporary housing if a covered loss makes the apartment uninhabitable
- Personal liability if you are legally responsible for injury or property damage
- Medical payments to others for limited guest injuries, regardless of fault

The [Virginia renters insurance consumer guide](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/consumers/insurance/property-casualty-consumer/renters-insurance-guide/) is the official statewide explanation of those building blocks. Use it to understand the form. Use this page to understand why Arlington leases and buildings make those building blocks matter.

## Lease requirements in high-rises and garden apartments

Virginia does not mandate renters insurance. Arlington property managers often do. A lease may require a minimum liability limit, proof of coverage at move-in, and the landlord or management company listed as an additional interested party. That listing protects the landlord's interest in your liability coverage. It does not extend the landlord's building policy to your belongings.

High-rise leases along Metro corridors and garden-apartment leases can both include this clause. The building type still changes what you should inventory:

- High-rise and mid-rise units: stacked living means a leak, overflow, or fire can affect neighbors above or below you. Liability and loss of use deserve a careful look.
- Garden apartments: more ground-level access, shared courtyards, and storage rooms can change theft and water conversations. Basement or exterior storage is easy to forget on an inventory.
- Townhome-style rentals: you still do not insure the structure, but outdoor furniture, bikes, and detached storage may need an explicit discussion with an agent.

Ask the property manager what proof they want (declarations page, interested-party wording) and keep a copy when you renew.

## Flood near Four Mile Run and the Potomac lowlands

Standard renters policies typically exclude flood. Flood is rising water from outside the building, including overflow from a stream, river, or drainage system. That is not the same as a burst supply line or an overflowing washing machine, which may be treated as a different peril.

Parts of Arlington sit near Four Mile Run and the Potomac lowlands. That does not mean every building is in a mapped high-risk zone, and this page does not assign a flood score to a neighborhood. It means you should look up the building on an official flood map before you assume an HO-4 policy is enough.

[FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) explains National Flood Insurance Program contents coverage. Contents flood insurance is purchased separately from the building coverage a landlord might carry. Auto comprehensive is also not flood insurance for an apartment.

Do not treat a renters declarations page as proof of flood coverage.

## Transit, bikes, and belongings that move with you

Orange, Silver, and Blue Line access is part of why people rent in Arlington. A renters policy is not travel insurance, but the belongings you take in and out of a high-rise (laptops, bikes, packages left with a concierge or in a locker) should appear on your inventory. High-value bikes and jewelry often sit under sub-limits unless you schedule them.

Loss of use can matter more in a tight inner-core rental market than in a place with more short-term vacancies. If a covered fire or water loss takes your unit offline, the policy may help with hotel and related costs up to the limit you bought. Confirm how the carrier defines "uninhabitable" and what receipts they want.

If you also drive, Virginia auto rules are separate. See [Virginia auto insurance requirements](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/requirements/) and, for a nearby independent-city commuting pattern, [Alexandria auto insurance](/states/virginia/alexandria/).

## Coverage combinations that matter in this county

Discuss these combinations with a licensed Virginia producer. They are not a shopping list of required add-ons:

- Replacement cost versus actual cash value for personal property, especially if you own recent electronics and furniture
- Liability limits that meet the lease and still reflect guests, pets, and stacked-unit water risk
- Scheduled personal property for items above standard sub-limits
- Water or sewer backup endorsements, which are often excluded unless added
- Separate contents flood coverage if the building's map location warrants a closer look
- Roommate naming: one policy with every occupant listed, or a policy for each unrelated roommate

Walk the unit, list what you own, and match the lease language. Do not take a coverage number from this page.

## Questions to ask a licensed professional

- Does this policy meet the liability minimum and interested-party wording in my Arlington lease?
- Are my roommates named, or do they need their own policies?
- Is personal property written as replacement cost or actual cash value?
- Based on this building's location, should I look at contents flood coverage in addition to HO-4?
- Would a water or sewer backup endorsement apply to this high-rise or garden-apartment plumbing?
- If I also garage a car in Arlington, how should renters and auto policies be coordinated?

A licensed insurance professional authorized in Virginia can review the lease clause and the unit, then explain what the form actually covers. Arlington's density and Metro adjacency change the facts on the ground. They do not change which state's insurance department is in charge.
