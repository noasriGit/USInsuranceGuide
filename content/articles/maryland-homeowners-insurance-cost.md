Maryland does not set one official homeowners insurance price. Premiums vary by location, construction, replacement cost, deductibles, claims history, and other rating factors. **State law does not require homeowners insurance.** A mortgage lender typically does, and that contract rule is separate from the Insurance Article.

| Topic | What it means for cost |
|---|---|
| State-law requirement | Maryland does not mandate a homeowners policy by statute. |
| Lender or association rule | A mortgage or condo association can still require coverage. |
| Main price drivers | Location, wind exposure, rebuild cost, deductibles, claims, and credit-based scores where permitted. |
| Flood | Usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy. |
| How to shop | Compare equivalent limits and deductibles. Do not use an invented statewide average. |

For coverages, exclusions, and regional risk notes, see [Maryland homeowners insurance](/states/maryland/homeowners-insurance/). Neighboring [Virginia homeowners insurance](/states/virginia/homeowners-insurance/) follows the same state-law versus lender split, with different coastal and Northern Virginia pricing patterns.

## Maryland State Law Versus Lender Requirements

A cost conversation should start with **who is requiring the policy**.

**Maryland statute** does not require a homeowner to buy homeowners insurance. You can own a home outright without a state-issued homeowners mandate.

**Mortgage lenders** typically require a policy as a condition of the loan. The lender usually must be listed as mortgagee, and dwelling coverage must meet the lender's minimum, which may be tied to the loan amount or to replacement cost. If the policy lapses, the lender may force-place insurance. Force-placed coverage is often more expensive and more limited than a policy you choose.

**Condominium associations** may require an HO-6 unit owners policy even when the master policy covers the building exterior. That is an association or bylaw requirement, not a Maryland insurance statute. See [condo insurance](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/) for how unit owners coverage differs from a standard homeowners form.

If you have no mortgage and no association rule, a policy is still a risk-transfer choice for the dwelling, belongings, and liability. It is not a state filing requirement.

## What Affects Maryland Homeowners Insurance Premiums

Insurers price the chance that they will pay a dwelling, contents, additional-living-expense, or liability claim. The [Maryland Insurance Administration homeowners page](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/homeownersinsurance.aspx) is the official consumer starting point for coverage questions. Rating details vary by company. Common factors include:

| Factor | How it can move premium |
|---|---|
| Location | Fire protection, crime, building codes, and nearby claims experience all differ by ZIP and county. |
| Coastal or wind exposure | Chesapeake Bay, Atlantic, and tidal properties may face higher wind pricing or percentage wind deductibles. |
| Construction and age | Older wiring, roofs, or non-standard construction can change rebuild cost and underwriting. |
| Replacement cost | A higher dwelling limit generally costs more because the insurer is promising a larger rebuild check. |
| Deductibles | A higher deductible usually lowers premium and raises your out-of-pocket share. |
| Claims history | Prior losses on the property or the policyholder can affect eligibility and price. |
| Credit-based insurance scores | Where permitted, these scores may be one rating variable. |
| Protective devices | Smoke alarms, burglar alarms, and sprinklers may qualify for company-specific discounts. |

This table is a comparison checklist, not a quote. Two houses with the same tax assessment can insure for very different amounts if rebuild cost, roof age, or wind deductibles differ.

## Replacement Cost Versus Actual Cash Value

How the policy values the dwelling and contents is one of the largest cost-and-gap decisions.

| Valuation method | What the insurer generally pays |
|---|---|
| Replacement cost | Repair or replace with similar materials at current prices, without subtracting depreciation (subject to limits and deductibles). |
| Actual cash value (ACV) | Replacement cost minus depreciation. |

Replacement cost coverage usually costs more than ACV because the insurer is not reducing the payout for age and wear. ACV can look cheaper at purchase and then leave a shortfall when you rebuild or replace belongings.

Dwelling limit should track **what it would cost to rebuild**, not the purchase price, tax assessment, or remaining mortgage. A limit set only to satisfy a lender's loan balance can be too low if construction costs in your Maryland county are higher than the loan.

Contents may also be written as replacement cost or ACV. Confirm that choice on the declarations page before you treat two quotes as equal.

## Coastal Wind Exposure and Deductible Design

Maryland is not one rating territory. Waterfront and tidal properties can see different wind terms than inland suburban homes. Ask whether the wind or hurricane deductible is a flat dollar amount or a percentage of dwelling coverage, whether it applies to all wind or only named storms, and whether roofs and outbuildings use the same deductible. A percentage deductible can lower the quoted premium and still leave a large bill after a coastal storm.

Inland Maryland homes still face thunderstorm wind, winter storms, and plumbing water claims. Those risks show up in claims history and in the endorsements you add or skip.

## Flood Exclusion and Separate Flood Insurance

A standard homeowners policy typically **excludes flood**. Flood generally means water that rises from the ground or overflows from a body of water, including storm surge and surface water. That exclusion is one of the most common coverage gaps in Maryland, including properties near the Chesapeake Bay, rivers, and low-lying inland areas.

Flood insurance is a separate product, often through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood insurer. FEMA's [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) site explains building versus contents coverage and waiting periods. For the policy contrast, see [home insurance versus flood insurance](/blog/home-insurance-vs-flood-insurance/) and the [flood insurance hub](/flood-insurance/).

A lender can require flood insurance in a mapped high-risk zone even though Maryland does not require a homeowners policy by statute. Those are two different contracts. Removing flood coverage to lower a combined monthly escrow is not the same as shopping a homeowners deductible.

## Condo Insurance Versus Homeowners Insurance Cost

An HO-3 style homeowners policy prices the dwelling you own. An HO-6 condo policy prices interior improvements, personal property, and liability, while the association master policy usually covers shared building elements.

That split changes the premium comparison:

- Do not compare a condo quote to a single-family dwelling quote as if they were the same product
- Ask what the master policy deductible is, because a unit owner may still need loss-assessment or interior coverage
- Confirm whether the association requires specific liability limits or loss-assessment amounts

Use the [condo insurance guide](/home-insurance/condo-insurance/) when the building is a condominium. Use this page when you own a house or townhome that needs dwelling coverage.

## Ways to Reduce a Maryland Homeowners Premium Without Creating Gaps

Change rating inputs you understand, not coverages you still need. You can ask about a deductible you can actually pay, protective devices the insurer credits, multi-policy pricing, an updated rebuild-cost worksheet, and roof or plumbing updates the underwriter recognizes. This site does not invent savings amounts.

Avoid lowering the dwelling limit below a realistic rebuild cost, switching to ACV without understanding depreciation, dropping liability to a token amount, assuming flood or sewer backup is included, or letting the policy lapse into force-placed coverage. The [MIA homeowners and renters page](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/pages/homeownersandrentersinsurance.aspx) can help you frame those questions.

## How to Compare Maryland Homeowners Quotes

A useful comparison uses the same house and the same coverage design:

1. Start with a current **rebuild-cost estimate**, not the tax bill.
2. Align **dwelling, other structures, contents, loss of use, and liability** limits.
3. Align **all-peril and wind deductibles**.
4. List **endorsements** you want on every quote, such as sewer backup or ordinance or law, if you need them.
5. Confirm each company is **licensed in Maryland**.
6. Read exclusions for flood, earth movement, and maintenance-related water.

If a quote is much lower, find the missing limit, higher deductible, or ACV clause before you assume you found a bargain. This site does not rank carriers and does not publish invented Maryland averages.

## Questions to Ask a Licensed Professional About Maryland Homeowners Cost

- Is the dwelling limit based on replacement cost in my county, or on the loan balance?
- What is my wind or hurricane deductible in dollars after a storm?
- Am I comparing replacement cost to actual cash value without realizing it?
- Does my lender or association require anything the state does not require?
- Do I need separate [flood insurance](/flood-insurance/) based on this property's exposure?
- If I own a condo, should I be shopping an HO-6 instead of a homeowners form?

A licensed Maryland agent or broker can price the same rebuild-cost number across companies and explain which savings come from a real discount and which come from a thinner policy.
