If you garage or register a vehicle in Alexandria, Virginia, you must meet Virginia auto insurance rules. For policies effective January 1, 2025 or later, that means 50/100/25 liability and matching uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage unless you reject UM/UIM in writing. Alexandria is an independent city. City hall does not set those limits. The [Virginia Bureau of Insurance](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/consumers/insurance/) and [Virginia DMV](https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/) do.

This page is about what is locally different: Old Town streets, I-95 / I-495 / Route 1 commuting, Blue and Yellow Line Metro access, and street parking versus urban garages. It does not invent crash rates or city-specific premiums. For the statewide legal floor, use the [Virginia auto insurance requirements guide](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/requirements/) and the [Virginia auto insurance overview](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/).

## An independent city with a mixed driving pattern

Alexandria is not a neighborhood of Arlington and not a suburb of Fairfax County. It is its own city, with a historic Old Town grid, waterfront streets, and later corridors that feed I-95, the Capital Beltway (I-495), and Route 1. Blue and Yellow Line Metro stations sit inside the city, so some households drive every day and others use the car mainly for weekends or for the first mile to a station.

That mix matters for how an insurer may look at garaging and mileage. It does not create a separate Alexandria insurance code. The [City of Alexandria website](https://www.alexandriava.gov/) is the place for local government services. It is not the place for minimum liability limits.

If you are comparing nearby localities, [Arlington renters insurance](/states/virginia/arlington/) covers a denser county rental market, and [Fairfax renters insurance](/states/virginia/fairfax/) covers an independent city surrounded by Fairfax County. Drivers who also keep a Maryland commute should read [Rockville auto insurance](/states/maryland/rockville/) so they do not confuse 50/100/25 with Maryland's 30/60/15.

## Virginia rules that actually apply

Virginia requires liability insurance on registered vehicles. The former uninsured motor vehicle fee ended July 1, 2024. You cannot register an Alexandria vehicle as uninsured by paying DMV a fee.

Minimum liability for policies effective January 1, 2025 or later:

| Coverage | Minimum limit |
|---|---|
| Bodily injury, one person | $50,000 |
| Bodily injury, per accident | $100,000 |
| Property damage, per accident | $25,000 |

Those figures are commonly written 50/100/25. Older policies issued under the prior 30/60/20 floor should be checked at renewal.

Virginia also requires uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage at matching minimums unless you reject that coverage in writing. UM may apply when the other driver has no insurance or cannot be identified. UIM may apply when the at-fault driver has insurance that is too low. A written rejection is a real choice with a real gap. It is not a default setting you should click through.

Virginia is a fault-based state. It does not require personal injury protection (PIP). Medical payments (MedPay) may be offered as an optional add-on. That is a different design from [Maryland auto insurance requirements](/states/maryland/auto-insurance/requirements/), where insurers must offer PIP.

## Street parking, Old Town, and urban garages

Old Town's historic streets are narrow, with a mix of on-street spaces and nearby garages. Other parts of the city use apartment or condo garages, surface lots, and street parking near Metro. None of those arrangements change the legal minimums. They do change the optional coverages people should ask about:

- Collision may pay to repair your own vehicle after a crash, including low-speed contact in tight blocks, alleys, or garage ramps.
- Comprehensive (other than collision) may apply to theft, vandalism, falling objects, and certain weather events while the car is parked.
- Rental reimbursement may matter if you depend on the car for an I-95 or Beltway commute and the vehicle is in the shop after a covered loss.

State law does not require collision or comprehensive. A lender or lessor often does. If the car is older and owned outright, the tradeoff is between premium and the cost of replacing the vehicle yourself.

This page does not publish Alexandria crash statistics or neighborhood theft rates. Those figures are easy to invent and hard to defend. Ask your agent how garaging address, parking type, and annual mileage are used on the application, and answer those questions accurately.

## Commuting across I-95, I-495, and Route 1

Many Alexandria trips are not local errands. They are corridor trips on I-95, I-495, or Route 1, or a drive to a Blue or Yellow Line station. Higher commuting mileage can affect rating. Crossing into Maryland or the District does not let you carry only the other jurisdiction's minimums on a Virginia-registered vehicle.

If you work in Maryland, you still need a Virginia-compliant policy on a car registered here. Maryland's 30/60/15 floor is lower than Virginia's current 50/100/25 floor. Meeting Maryland's number is not enough for a Virginia registration. If you work in the District, read [Washington, D.C. auto insurance](/states/washington-dc/auto-insurance/) as a contrast, not as the rule that governs your Alexandria garage.

Flood is a separate conversation from auto comprehensive. Comprehensive is not a substitute for flood insurance on a home or rental. For rising water at a residence, see [FloodSmart](https://www.floodsmart.gov/).

## Lapses, proof, and SR-22

Virginia DMV verifies insurance electronically. If coverage cannot be confirmed, license, registration, and plates may be suspended. Getting back on the road often means a $600 noncompliance fee, proof of future financial responsibility (commonly an SR-22 for three years), and additional reinstatement fees.

Carry proof of insurance when you drive. If you switch carriers, ask both companies how the electronic record is updated so a one-day gap does not look like a lapse.

For the full penalty and proof walkthrough, use the [Virginia auto insurance requirements page](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/requirements/). For the state hub, use [Virginia insurance guides](/states/virginia/).

## Coverage combinations that matter in Alexandria

Talk through these combinations with a licensed Virginia producer. They are discussion points, not a city ordinance:

- Liability at or above 50/100/25, based on assets and how you use the car
- UM/UIM kept or rejected in writing, with a clear explanation of the gap
- Collision and comprehensive if you park on the street, in a shared garage, or finance the vehicle
- MedPay if you want some medical coverage that does not depend on proving the other driver's fault
- Deductibles you can actually pay after a garage or street incident
- Accurate garaging ZIP and commute mileage, including Beltway and I-95 trips

If you also rent in the city, renters insurance is a separate Virginia contract. It is not required by statute, but a lease may require it. Nearby rental context lives on the [Arlington renters insurance page](/states/virginia/arlington/).

- Does my current policy meet 50/100/25 for a 2025 or later effective date?
- What happens if I sign a written UM/UIM rejection?
- How should I describe street parking versus a garage on the application?
- Do collision and comprehensive make sense for this car and this parking pattern?
- If I commute into Maryland or D.C., do I still meet Virginia registration rules?
- What records does DMV need if I change insurers mid-term?
