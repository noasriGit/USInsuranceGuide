Virginia does not publish one official car insurance price that applies to every driver. The [Virginia Bureau of Insurance](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/consumers/insurance/) states that Virginia has a **competitive rating law**, which means licensed companies can charge different premiums for the same household. What you pay depends on your record, vehicle, garaging ZIP code, mileage, limits, deductibles, and other rating factors. Virginia's 50/100/25 liability minimum is a **legal floor**, not a recommended limit and not a statewide average.

| What you are asking | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Is there an official statewide average premium? | No. Virginia uses competitive rating. Compare equivalent quotes. |
| What is the legal liability floor for 2025+ policies? | 50/100/25, plus UM/UIM at least at those minimums. |
| Do you need insurance to register? | Yes. Certify coverage before DMV issues plates. |
| What should you not drop to save money? | Required liability and required UM/UIM. Reducing extra UM/UIM is not the same as dropping it. |
| Who explains shopping rules? | The [Bureau of Insurance](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/consumers/insurance/) and [Virginia DMV](https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/vehicles/insurance-requirements). |

This page is about pricing, rating, and comparison. For the legal checklist, use [Virginia auto insurance requirements](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/requirements/). For the statewide hub, see [Virginia auto insurance](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/).

## What Virginia Legally Requires Versus What You Pay For

Virginia law decides whether a policy is legal. It does not assign you a premium. [Virginia DMV](https://www.dmv.virginia.gov/vehicles/insurance-requirements) requires a liability policy from a company authorized to do business in Virginia on every registered vehicle.

For policies effective January 1, 2025 or later, a standard policy must include:

- **Liability** of at least $50,000 bodily injury per person, $100,000 bodily injury per accident, and $25,000 property damage per accident (50/100/25)
- **Uninsured and underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage at least at those statutory minimums. UM/UIM generally matches the policy's liability limits. A named insured may reduce additional UM/UIM, but cannot eliminate it.

Collision, comprehensive (other than collision), medical expense benefits, loss of income benefits, and rental reimbursement are optional under state law. They change premium because they change the insurer's exposure. A lender or lessor can still require physical-damage coverage in a finance or lease contract.

If you also keep a vehicle in [Maryland](/states/maryland/auto-insurance/cost/), Maryland's floor is 30/60/15 with matching UM/UIM and a PIP offer. A Virginia-registered vehicle still has to meet Virginia rules.

## What Affects Virginia Car Insurance Cost

The Bureau of Insurance oversees licensed auto insurers. Each company uses its own rating plan. Common inputs include:

| Rating factor | Why it can change premium |
|---|---|
| Driving record | At-fault accidents and convictions can signal higher claim risk. The Bureau of Insurance notes that related premium increases may be applied for 36 months. |
| Age and experience | Newer or younger drivers are often priced differently from long-tenured drivers. |
| Household drivers | Virginia DMV notes that insurers may consider the driving record of any driving-age person in the household. |
| Vehicle | Repair cost, theft exposure, safety equipment, and how the car is used all matter. |
| Garaging ZIP code | Northern Virginia addresses, including Alexandria, can rate differently from rural ZIP codes. The legal minimum does not change. |
| Annual mileage and use | Pleasure use, a short commute, carpooling, or driving under 7,500 miles a year are examples the Bureau of Insurance lists as facts that may qualify for a lower premium if they are true. |
| Limits and deductibles | Higher limits and lower deductibles generally cost more. |
| Credit-based insurance scores | Where permitted, insurers may use credit-based scores as one rating variable. |

No single factor is a quote by itself. Two households on the same street can receive different prices after an insurer reviews drivers, vehicles, and coverage elections.

For renewal increases that are not tied to one ticket, see [why car insurance may go up](/blog/why-did-my-car-insurance-go-up/).

## Why Two Virginia Drivers May Receive Very Different Quotes

Competitive rating is the main reason a published "average Virginia rate" is a poor substitute for a quote.

One driver may have a clean record, a short commute, and liability-only coverage on an older car. Another may add a newly licensed household driver, garage the car in a denser ZIP code, finance a newer vehicle that requires collision and comprehensive, and buy higher liability limits. Both can still meet 50/100/25. They should not expect the same price.

Company underwriting standards also differ. The Bureau of Insurance tells consumers that a turndown at one company is not a turndown at every company, and that premiums and underwriting rules are not identical.

## How to Compare Virginia Auto Quotes Correctly

Comparison works when every quote describes the same risk and the same coverage. It fails when you mix six-month and annual prices, forget a household driver, or treat an unofficial "average Virginia rate" as your price.

1. List the same vehicles, drivers, and garaging address on every application.
2. Lock liability, UM/UIM limits, deductibles, and optional physical damage before you shop.
3. Ask whether each price is for **six months or one year**. The Bureau of Insurance notes that many companies renew every six months.
4. Include installment, membership, or agent service fees that sit outside the base premium.
5. Request written quotes from more than one insurer licensed in Virginia.
6. Match the declarations line by line, including listed discounts.

The Bureau of Insurance publishes a [2025 Auto Insurance Consumer Guide](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/media/sccvirginiagov-home/consumer-home/insurance/property-amp-casualty/automobile-insurance/autoguide.pdf) and a shopper's worksheet. Those tools help you line up coverages. They are not a price list. If you cannot obtain coverage in the standard market, a licensed agent can place you in the **Virginia Automobile Insurance Plan**. The Bureau of Insurance describes that plan as a last resort because premiums are often higher than those of voluntary companies.

If you want help lining up equivalent quotes, [get insurance help](/get-insurance-help/).

## Required Versus Optional Coverage

**Required liability** keeps the vehicle legal. It pays others when you are at fault, subject to the policy. It does not repair your car or pay your own medical bills.

**UM/UIM** is required at least at the statutory minimums. It generally matches the policy's liability limits. Reducing additional UM/UIM toward 50/100/25 can change premium and also reduces first-party protection if an uninsured, underinsured, or unidentified hit-and-run driver causes a loss. UM/UIM cannot be eliminated. For hit-and-run property damage, Virginia law requires you to pay the first $200 before UM property damage coverage applies.

**Collision and comprehensive** are optional under statute. They are the coverages people often mean by "full coverage," which is not a Virginia legal category. Collision may pay for your vehicle after a crash. Comprehensive may pay for theft, vandalism, hail, or hitting an animal, subject to the deductible.

**Medical expense and loss of income benefits** are optional. Virginia is a fault-based state and does not require personal injury protection (PIP).

Do not drop required liability to chase a lower quote. If a lender requires physical-damage coverage, dropping collision or comprehensive can violate the loan even when Virginia statute does not require those coverages.

## Deductibles and Coverage-Limit Tradeoffs

A deductible is the amount you pay first on a covered collision or comprehensive claim. The Bureau of Insurance explains that a higher deductible usually lowers premium and raises your share after a loss. Choose an amount you can fund without hardship. The savings between two deductible choices may not be large enough to justify the extra out-of-pocket risk.

Raising liability or UM/UIM above 50/100/25 generally increases premium because the insurer is promising a larger payment if you cause a serious injury or property claim. 50/100/25 satisfies DMV. It is not a statement that those dollars will replace another person's vehicle or pay a large medical claim.

This page does not recommend a specific limit above the legal floor.

## Discounts That May Be Available

Discounts are company-specific. This site does not invent dollar amounts or percentage savings.

The Bureau of Insurance lists facts that may entitle you to a lower premium **if they apply to you**, including:

- Anti-theft devices, automatic seat belts, and/or air bags
- Pleasure use only, or a short commute
- Carpooling with limited drive-to-work days
- Good-student status for a full-time student, generally a B average or above
- Driving less than 7,500 miles per year
- No accidents or violations
- Completing a driver education or defensive driving course

Virginia law also requires insurers to give a premium reduction to people **age 55 or older** who successfully complete a DMV-approved motor vehicle accident prevention course. Each insurer sets the amount. The qualification lasts three years after the course. Insurers may also make an age-based reduction for insureds over 55.

Ask the producer which discounts appear on the quote and what could remove them at renewal. A discount that requires a telematics program, a clean record, or a bundled homeowners policy is not automatic.

## Why Premiums May Increase at Renewal

A higher renewal price is not always a new ticket. Common reasons include:

- An at-fault accident or a conviction that the insurer can rate, often for up to 36 months
- A new household driver, including a newly licensed driver
- A vehicle, mileage, or garaging-address change
- A lapse, which Virginia DMV warns can lead to higher premiums for the next three years
- The company filing new rates for its book of business

If you believe a surcharge was applied for an accident that was not your fault, the Bureau of Insurance says you may ask it to review the increase.

Changing companies mid-term can add cancellation costs. Shop before renewal when you can. Virginia law also allows a new insurer to cancel during the first 60 days, so disclose your record when you apply.

## How Virginia Differs From Maryland and D.C. on Cost

The legal floor is not the same, so a neighboring quote is not a Virginia quote.

| Jurisdiction | Liability floor | UM / UIM | PIP | Cost page |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Virginia** | 50/100/25 (2025+) | Required at least at 50/100/25; extra UM/UIM may be reduced | Not required | This page |
| **Maryland** | 30/60/15 | Required at matching minimums | Offer required; written waiver allowed | [Maryland car insurance cost](/states/maryland/auto-insurance/cost/) |
| **Washington, D.C.** | 25/50/10 | Required | Offer required; you may decline | No standalone D.C. cost page in this publishing pass |

A denser Northern Virginia ZIP code can rate differently from a suburban Maryland ZIP even when the drivers look similar on paper. That is a rating difference. It does not let a Virginia-registered vehicle carry only Maryland or D.C. minimums.

## Questions to Ask a Licensed Professional About Virginia Auto Cost

- Do these quotes use the same vehicles, drivers, ZIP code, limits, deductibles, and policy term?
- Is 50/100/25 only meeting the legal floor, and what changes if I raise liability or UM/UIM?
- Which discounts am I eligible for today, including the age-55 accident-prevention discount if it applies?
- If I buy liability above 50/100/25, what happens to premium and first-party protection if I reduce UM/UIM toward the statutory minimum?
- Are collision and comprehensive required by my lender or lease?
- What happens to my premium and my registration if coverage lapses even for a short period?

A licensed Virginia producer can price equivalent coverage and keep the comparison tied to your household rather than to an invented statewide average.
