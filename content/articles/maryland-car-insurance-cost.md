Maryland does not publish one official car insurance price that applies to every driver. What you pay depends on your record, vehicle, garaging ZIP code, mileage, limits, deductibles, personal injury protection (PIP) choice, and other rating factors. Maryland's 30/60/15 liability minimum is a **legal floor**, not a recommended limit.

| What you are asking | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Is there an official statewide average premium? | No. Compare equivalent quotes from licensed insurers. |
| Is there an official monthly average? | No. Confirm whether a quote is for six months or a year before dividing. |
| What is the legal liability floor? | 30/60/15, plus matching UM/UIM. |
| Does PIP affect cost? | Insurers must offer at least $2,500 in PIP. You may waive it in writing. |

## What Maryland Requires Versus What You Pay For

Maryland law decides whether a policy is legal. It does not decide what a specific insurer will charge you. A standard Maryland auto policy must include:

- **Liability** of at least $30,000 bodily injury per person, $60,000 bodily injury per accident, and $15,000 property damage per accident (30/60/15)
- **Uninsured and underinsured motorist (UM/UIM)** coverage that matches those minimums
- A **PIP offer of at least $2,500**, which you may accept, limit, or waive in writing

Collision, comprehensive, rental reimbursement, and roadside assistance are optional under state law. They change premium because they change the insurer's exposure.

If you also keep a vehicle in [Virginia](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/cost/), Virginia's floor for policies effective January 1, 2025 or later is 50/100/25, with UM/UIM unless rejected in writing. A Maryland-registered vehicle still has to meet Maryland rules. See [Virginia auto insurance requirements](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/requirements/).

## Why 30/60/15 Is a Legal Floor, Not a Recommended Limit

30/60/15 is the smallest liability package Maryland accepts. It is not a statement that those dollars will pay a serious injury claim or replace another person's vehicle.

Buying only the minimum can lower a quoted premium and still leave a gap if medical bills, lost wages, or property damage exceed the limits. Matching UM/UIM at the same minimums can leave a similar gap if you are hit by a driver with little or no coverage. Raising limits generally increases premium. Whether that tradeoff makes sense depends on your assets and risk tolerance. This page does not recommend a specific limit above the legal floor.

## What Affects Maryland Car Insurance Rates

Insurers licensed in Maryland use rating plans that the Maryland Insurance Administration oversees. The exact weight of each factor varies by company. Common inputs include:

| Rating factor | Why it can change premium |
|---|---|
| Driving record | At-fault accidents, violations, and lapses can signal higher claim risk. |
| Age and experience | Newer or younger drivers are often priced differently from long-tenured drivers. |
| Vehicle | Repair cost, theft exposure, safety features, and how the car is used all matter. |
| Garaging ZIP code | Local accident, theft, and medical-cost patterns can differ from one Maryland ZIP to the next. |
| Annual mileage | More time on the road can mean more exposure to claims. |
| Limits and deductibles | Higher limits and lower deductibles generally cost more. |
| PIP election | Full PIP, limited PIP, or a written waiver changes the coverage you are buying. |
| Credit-based insurance scores | Where permitted, insurers may use credit-based scores as one rating variable. |

No single factor is a quote by itself. Two households on the same street can receive different prices after an insurer reviews drivers, vehicles, and coverage elections.

## Why is car insurance expensive in Maryland?

Maryland does not publish a finding that car insurance is objectively expensive for every driver. A quote can still feel high when several rating inputs stack together. Factors that **may** raise a Maryland premium, without applying to every household, include:

- **Claims frequency** in the area where the car is garaged
- **Vehicle repair costs**, including parts and labor for the specific model
- **Medical costs** that affect bodily injury claims
- **Garaging location**, including close-in Montgomery County ZIP codes versus other parts of the state
- **Theft or vandalism exposure** for the vehicle and parking arrangement
- **Driving record**, including at-fault accidents, violations, and lapses
- **Coverage selections**, including limits, deductibles, and PIP election
- **Annual mileage** and how the car is used
- **Insurer-specific rating models**, which can price the same household differently from one licensed company to the next

None of those factors is a statewide average, and none is a claim that Maryland is always more expensive than Virginia or Washington, D.C. Compare equivalent quotes. For neighboring rate-factor context, see [Virginia car insurance cost](/states/virginia/auto-insurance/cost/).

## How to Compare Maryland Auto Policies Without Invented Quotes

Comparison works when every quote describes the same risk and the same coverage. It fails when you mix limits, forget a driver, or treat an unofficial "average Maryland rate" as your price.

1. List the same vehicles, drivers, and garaging address on every application.
2. Lock liability, UM/UIM, deductibles, optional physical damage, and PIP choice before you shop.
3. Request written quotes from more than one insurer licensed in Maryland.
4. Match the declarations line by line, including listed discounts.
5. Ask what changes the renewal price: a new household driver, a usage change, or a lapse.

The [Maryland Auto Insurance Guide](https://insurance.maryland.gov/consumer/documents/publications/autoinsuranceguide.pdf) explains coverages. It is not a price list. If you want help lining up equivalent quotes, [get insurance help](/get-insurance-help/).

## Discounts That May Lower a Maryland Auto Premium

Discounts are company-specific. Maryland does not publish a required discount schedule, and this site does not invent dollar amounts or percentage savings.

Discounts you may ask about in general terms include:

- **Multi-policy or multi-vehicle** pricing when you place more than one policy or vehicle with the same insurer
- **Good driver or claims-free** pricing after a period without chargeable accidents or violations
- **Safety and anti-theft features** that the insurer recognizes in its rating plan
- **Payment or paperless** options, if the insurer offers them
- **Student or mature-driver** programs, when you actually qualify

Ask the producer to show which discounts appear on the quote and what could remove them at renewal. A discount that requires a telematics program, a clean record, or a bundled homeowners policy is not automatic.

## Coverages You Should Not Drop Just to Lower Cost

Cutting premium by removing coverage only works if the remaining policy still meets Maryland law and still matches the risk you are willing to keep.

**Do not drop required liability.** Driving or registering a vehicle without 30/60/15 liability is not a legal savings strategy.

**Do not drop required UM/UIM.** Maryland pairs uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage with your liability minimums. Removing it is not an available cost shortcut on a standard policy.

**Be careful with PIP.** Waiving or limiting PIP may reduce premium, but it changes how medical expenses and lost wages are handled after an accident. Maryland requires an affirmative written waiver on the required form if you decline the offer. Read the notice before you sign.

**Collision and comprehensive are optional under state law**, but a lender or lessor can require them in a finance or lease contract. Dropping them on a financed vehicle can violate that contract even if Maryland statute does not require those coverages.

**Raising deductibles** can lower premium, but it increases what you pay out of pocket after a covered claim. Choose a deductible you can actually fund.

## Maryland Auto Insurance Lapse Fees and Continuous Coverage

Cost planning includes the cost of a gap. Maryland uses electronic insurance verification. If the Motor Vehicle Administration cannot confirm coverage, you can face:

- A **$150** penalty for the first 30 days without verified insurance
- **$7** for each additional day
- Possible **registration suspension**
- An **FR-19** certificate showing continuous coverage before reinstatement

Those fees are separate from any higher premium an insurer may charge after a lapse. Maintaining continuous coverage is both a legal requirement and a practical way to avoid compounding costs. Details are on the [MVA insurance requirements page](https://mva.maryland.gov/title-registration/auto-insurance-requirements).

## Questions to Ask a Licensed Professional About Maryland Auto Cost

- Do these quotes use the same vehicles, drivers, ZIP code, limits, deductibles, and PIP election?
- Is 30/60/15 only meeting the legal floor, and what changes if I raise liability or UM/UIM?
- Which discounts am I eligible for today, and which ones depend on a clean record or a bundled policy?
- If I waive or limit PIP, what medical and wage gaps does that create?
- What happens to my premium and my registration if coverage lapses even for a short period?
- Are collision and comprehensive required by my lender or lease, even though Maryland statute does not require them?

A licensed Maryland producer can price equivalent coverage, explain PIP waiver forms, and keep the comparison tied to your household rather than to an invented statewide average.
